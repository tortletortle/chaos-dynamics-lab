# V0.5 Engineering and Experiment Report

## Objective

Connect the V0.4 independent-wheel suspension model to a bounded feedback controller and compare morphology/control search strategies under equal candidate-evaluation budgets.

## Implemented model

The V0.5 controlled simulator preserves the V0.4 equations when no controller is supplied. A regression case on the washboard terrain reproduced the V0.4 score and terminal reason exactly (score delta 0).

The optional controller returns a shared throttle command in [0, 1]. Its policy uses:
- forward speed error relative to a target speed;
- absolute chassis pitch;
- pitch-rate magnitude when pitch is diverging;
- mean estimated wheel slip above a configurable limit.

This is not reinforcement learning. It is a small interpretable feedback policy whose parameters can be evolved.

## Search methods

Four methods were compared:
1. Random search over morphology and controller parameters.
2. NSGA-II over morphology with full-throttle control fixed.
3. NSGA-II over controller parameters with the V0.4 base morphology fixed.
4. Joint NSGA-II over morphology and controller parameters.

NSGA-II uses five maximization objectives: mean training score, goal rate, negative energy use, negative mean pitch and negative score standard deviation. All four methods use 48 candidate evaluations per seed. The fixed baseline is reported separately and is not a search-budget competitor.

## Experiment protocol

- Search seeds: 7, 19, 41, 59, 73.
- Training scenarios: rough (31), ridge (47), washboard (83).
- Held-out scenarios: rough (101), ridge (113), washboard (127), low-grip (149).
- Search budget: 48 candidates per method and seed.
- Training integration step: 1/240 s.
- Validation: compare 1/480 s and 1/960 s. If terminal reason differs, score delta exceeds 0.05, fine energy residual exceeds 0.002, or dissipation becomes materially negative, run 1/1920 s and require agreement with the 1/960 s result.

Total: 960 search evaluations, 2,880 training simulations, 15 fixed-baseline training simulations and 202 held-out validation simulations.

## Results

| Method | Mean training score | Mean held-out score | Held-out goal rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Random search | 20.25 | 17.29 | 75% |
| Morphology-only NSGA-II | 19.79 | 16.24 | 80% |
| Controller-only NSGA-II | 12.67 | 14.08 | 40% |
| Joint NSGA-II | 20.34 | 16.08 | 70% |
| Fixed baseline | 11.81 | 13.13 | 50% |

All 20 held-out validations for each method passed the numerical consistency gate. One random-search case and one joint-search case used the 1920 Hz fallback.

Joint NSGA-II exceeded the fixed baseline in all five search-seed comparisons, but its mean held-out score was 1.20 points below random search. Morphology-only NSGA-II was also below random search by 1.05 points on average. The controller-only search improved the fixed baseline's mean score, but its held-out goal rate was lower.

## Interpretation

The current experiment does not support the claim that NSGA-II co-evolution is better than random search. A plausible explanation is that the search space is mixed discrete/continuous, the population and generation count are modest, and the shared-throttle controller has limited authority. These are hypotheses, not established causes.

The high training success rates alongside weaker held-out results indicate a generalization gap. The next experiment should:
- add more training terrain seeds and vary terrain parameters within families;
- keep a final untouched test set;
- report paired per-seed and per-terrain results, not only aggregate means;
- compare NSGA-II with a mixed-variable differential-evolution or random-plus-local-refinement baseline;
- inspect controller saturation and throttle traces;
- add wheel-specific torque allocation only after the shared-throttle model is audited.

## Limitations

This is a 2D reduced-order simulation, not a full multibody solver. The model lacks steering and lateral dynamics, explicit linkage geometry, full rigid-body contact, tire deformation, and physical calibration. The controller is a deterministic throttle policy, not PPO/SAC. Numerical convergence is a necessary check but cannot establish physical validity.
